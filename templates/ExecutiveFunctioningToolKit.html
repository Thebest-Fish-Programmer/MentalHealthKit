Pomodoro Technique

Helps with: Time Management

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method that uses a timer to break down work into intervals, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks.

Steps:

  1. Choose a task to work on.
  2. Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  3. Work on the task until the timer rings.
  4. Take a 5-minute break.
  5. Repeat steps 1-4 four times, then take a longer break (15-30 minutes).

How I plan on using it: I will use it in order to not burn myself out. This will keep me on task and focused.

Eat The Frog Method

Helps with: Task Management

The Eat The Frog method involves tackling the most difficult or important task first thing in the morning, before any distractions arise.

Steps:

  1. Identify your most challenging task of the day.
  2. Do it first thing in the morning, before checking email or social media.
  3. Work on it until it's completed or until you've made significant progress.

How I plan on using it: I will use this method in order to tackle the most difficult task first, which will help me stay focused and productive throughout the day.

Breathing

Helps with: Emotional Self-Control

Breathing can help activate your parasympathetic nervous system, which promotes relaxation and reduces stress. Which in stressful situations helps with emotional regulation. There are many ways to breath in order to sustain focus and calmness.

Techniques:

  • Box Breathing: Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, exhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds. Repeat.
  • 4-7-8 Breathing: Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7 seconds, exhale for 8 seconds. Repeat.
  • Diaphragmatic Breathing: Breathe deeply into your diaphragm (belly) rather than shallowly into your chest. Inhale deeply through your nose, allowing your belly to rise, then exhale slowly through your mouth.
  • Focus Breathing: Focus on your breath, observing each inhale and exhale without trying to control it. This helps anchor your attention in the present moment.

How I plan on using it: I will use this method in order to calm down so that I don't panic in stressful situations.

Movement & Stretching

Helps with: Response Self-Control

Movement and stretching can help improve focus, reduce stress, and increase energy levels. Regular physical activity can also help regulate mood and improve overall well-being.

Techniques:

  • Walking: Take a 5-minute walk every hour to break up long periods of sitting.
  • Stretching: Perform simple stretches to release tension in your muscles.
  • Desk Exercises: Do simple exercises at your desk to keep your body active.

How I plan on using it: I will use this method in order to stay active and reduce stress throughout the day.

Chunking

Helps with: Working Memory

Chunking is a memory technique that involves breaking down large pieces of information into smaller, manageable chunks. This makes it easier to remember and process information.

Steps:

  1. Identify the information you need to remember.
  2. Break it down into smaller, meaningful chunks.
  3. Group related chunks together.
  4. Use mnemonic devices or associations to help remember each chunk.

How I plan on using it: I will use this in order to remember information more easily.

Self-Reflection

Helps with: Metacognition

Self-reflection involves taking time to think about your thoughts, feelings, and actions. It helps you understand yourself better and make informed decisions.

Techniques:

  • Mindfulness: Practice mindfulness to stay present and aware of your thoughts and feelings.
  • Journaling: Write down your thoughts and feelings to gain insight into your experiences.
  • Goal Setting: Set goals for yourself and reflect on your progress regularly.

How I plan on using it: I will use this in order to gain a better understanding of my thoughts and feelings.

Plan B Technique

Helps with: Flexibility & Adaptability

The Plan B Technique involves having a backup plan in case your original plan doesn't work out. This helps you stay flexible and adapt to changing circumstances.

Steps:

  1. Identify your primary plan or goal.
  2. Consider potential obstacles or challenges that may arise.
  3. Create a backup plan (Plan B) that addresses these challenges.
  4. Be prepared to switch to Plan B if necessary.

How I plan on using it: I will use this in order to have a backup plan in case my original plan doesn't work out.

5-Minute Start

Helps with: Task Initiation

The 5-Minute Start technique involves committing to work on a task for just 5 minutes. This helps overcome procrastination and builds momentum to continue working.

Steps:

  1. Choose a task you need to work on.
  2. Set a timer for 5 minutes.
  3. Work on the task for the full 5 minutes, no matter what.
  4. After 5 minutes, decide whether to continue or take a break.

How I plan on using it: I will use this in order to overcome procrastination and build momentum to continue working.
